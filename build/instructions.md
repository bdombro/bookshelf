## 1. Cut

Follow the cutting list below and the diagrams in `sheets/`. With a plunge saw: lay each sheet on rigid foam insulation so both sides of every cut are supported, use about 2.6m of joined track for the full-length rips and 213cm crosscuts, and tape the cut lines or score first to stop splintering.
⚠️ The layout assumes a 3mm saw cut: check your blade's width and rebuild if it's wider.

## 3. Sand and finish

Sand both faces and visible edges of every pine panel, then apply the clear finish while panels are flat. Tape off glue areas: panel ends, the rear edges the back is glued to, and the shelf lines on the sides. Test on an offcut first.

For water-based varnish:

1. Wipe with a damp cloth to raise the grain; once dry, sand at 240.
2. Stir the can; don't shake it.
3. Roll an even coat with the wool mini roller; swap to a fresh sleeve when it starts to drag or shed.
4. Tip off at once: drag a near-dry wide synthetic brush lightly along the grain, end to end in one stroke on long panels.
5. Don't go back over areas that have started to set.
6. Brush the edges.
7. Coat both faces equally so panels don't cup.

Hardwax oil is wiped on thin and buffed off, not rolled.

## 5. Drill pocket holes

Uses: pocket hole jig.

Set the jig and the bit's depth collar to each panel's thickness: 24mm for bottom, top, cabinet_top, 18mm for shelf, kickboard, cabinet_shelf. Drill into each end of every panel that sits between the sides, on its hidden face: the underside of the bottom, cabinet top, cabinet shelf and open shelves, the top face of the top, the back face of the kickboard. At most 15cm apart. Positions from the panel's front edge:

- 25cm-deep panels: 6.2, 18.8
- 29.5cm-deep panels: 7.4, 22.1
- kickboard: 2 per end, at 2.5 and 7.5 from its bottom edge

## 6. Assemble each carcass

Uses: wood glue, 40 wood screws and 88 short wood screws, each with a washer, 64 pocket plugs.

Use the short screws in the thinner panels: a long one can break out through the outside face of the side.

Work on the floor with the sides on their rear edges, front edges up. A screw nudges its panel away from the pocket face as it tightens, so a shelf with pockets underneath creeps up off its line by a millimetre or so: clamp the panel to the side, or clamp a block on its line, before driving. In the narrow units, check a drill fits between the sides; if not, use a right-angle attachment or stubby driver.

1. Glue and screw the bottom and top between the two sides.
2. Add the cabinet top (wide) and the shelves, each on its marked line.
3. Fit the kickboard under the bottom, 5cm back from the front edge.
4. Leave the cabinet shelf until the back is on.
5. Glue plugs into the open shelves' pockets, sand them flush and varnish them.

## 7. Square and fix the back

Uses: 266 staples in total, wood glue.

Measure both diagonals of the carcass and push it until they match:

- narrow: 215.4
- wide: 228.8

Pencil the shelf lines on the back, then glue and staple it to the rear edges of the sides, top, bottom and every shelf, every 12.5cm. Keep each staple's crown along the edge, not across it, so a leg can't split out through a face. The back runs to the floor behind the kickboard.

## 8. Cabinet shelf, hinges and doors

Uses: 8 hinges, 4 knobs, short wood screws with washers.

1. Fix the cabinet shelf against the back with its underside at 47.9, leaving 4.5cm at the front for the hinges.
2. Drill each door's hinge cups 10cm from its top and bottom ends, using the hinge maker's template for the edge distance.
3. Mount the plates on the sides to match: door bottom at 10.2, top at 86.8.
4. Hang the doors and adjust for an even 0.4cm gap between the pair.
5. Fit each knob 4cm from the door's inner edge and 6cm down from its top.

## 9. Install

Uses: 8 connector bolts, 4 anti-tip brackets, 8 plasterboard toggles.

1. Remove the skirting boards.
2. Stand each unit up with two people; a wide unit is a two-person lift.
3. Bolt each set's two units together through their touching sides, 14.8cm from the front edge, at heights 30.1, 101.8, 133.2, 195.8.
4. Fix each unit's anti-tip bracket through its top panel into a stud wherever possible (find them with a stud finder). Where there's no stud use spring or snap toggles, never plain plugs. A narrow unit tips very easily: never skip this.
