## 3. Sand and finish

Sand both faces and visible edges of every pine panel, then apply the clear finish while panels are flat. Tape off glue areas: panel ends and the shelf lines on the sides and divider. Test on an offcut first.

For water-based varnish:

1. Wipe with a damp cloth to raise the grain; once dry, sand at 240.
2. Stir the can; don't shake it.
3. Roll an even coat with the wool mini roller; swap to a fresh sleeve when it starts to drag or shed.
4. Tip off at once: drag a near-dry wide synthetic brush lightly along the grain, end to end in one stroke on long panels.
5. Don't go back over areas that have started to set.
6. Brush the edges.
7. Coat both faces equally so panels don't cup.

Hardwax oil is wiped on thin and buffed off, not rolled.

Paint the backs' grooved face flat, before assembly: one coat of primer (0.4L), then one of black (0.4L). Brush into the grooves first, then roll the face with a spare sleeve, not the varnish one. The back is screwed, not glued, so nothing needs taping off.

## 5. Drill pocket holes

Uses: pocket hole jig.

Set the jig and the bit's depth collar to each panel's thickness: 37mm for top, 18mm for bottom, shelf, kickboard. Drill into each end of every panel that sits between a side and the divider (or, for the top, between the two sides), on its hidden face: the underside of the narrow bay's bottom and the open shelves, the top face of the top, the back face of the kickboard. The cabinet's bottom, cabinet top and cabinet shelf get corner braces instead, so no pockets. At most 15cm apart. Positions from the panel's front edge:

- 28.6cm-deep panels: 7.2, 21.5
- 29.5cm-deep panels: 7.4, 22.1
- kickboard: 2 per end, at 2.5 and 7.5 from its bottom edge

## 6. Assemble each carcass

Uses: wood glue, 12 wood screws and 88 short wood screws, each pocket screw with a washer, 64 pocket plugs, 24 corner braces.

Use the short screws in the 18mm panels and the long ones in the top. A screw nudges its panel away from the pocket face as it tightens, so a shelf with pockets underneath creeps up off its line by a millimetre or so: clamp the panel to the side, or clamp a block on its line, before driving. In the narrow bay, check a drill fits; if not, use a right-angle attachment or stubby driver.

Build it in the room it stands in. Work on the floor with the sides and divider on their front edges, rear edges up: every panel is flush at the front, and the sides stand 0.9cm proud at the back to frame the back panel.

1. Glue and screw the top between the two sides, then drive the long screws down through the top into the divider's end.
2. Glue and screw the narrow bay's bottom between its side and the divider, on its line. In the wide bay, screw corner braces to the side and divider with their top faces on the bottom's line, 2 per end, set back from the front edge so the doors and hinges clear them. Glue the bottom's ends, set it on the braces and screw up through them into it, with screws no longer than 15mm so they can't break through the 18mm panel.
3. Fix the cabinet top the same way, on braces under each end, then add the shelves on their marked lines with pocket screws.
4. Fit each kickboard under its bottom, 5cm back from the front edge.
5. Leave the cabinet shelf until the back is on.
6. Glue plugs into the open shelves' pockets, sand them flush and varnish them.

## 7. Square and fix the back

Uses: 120 back screws.

Measure both diagonals of the carcass and push it until they match: 241.4.

Pencil the shelf and divider lines on the back. Drop it in between the sides, under the top, painted face forward and grooves vertical. Drill and countersink, then screw through it into the rear edges of the divider, bottoms, cabinet top and every shelf, every 20cm. No glue. The back runs to the floor behind the kickboards.

## 8. Cabinet shelf, hinges and doors

Uses: 8 hinges, 4 knobs, corner braces.

1. Fix the cabinet shelf on 2 corner braces per end, against the back with its underside at 47.6, leaving 6.6cm at the front for the hinges; screw through the back into its rear edge too.
2. Drill each door's hinge cups 10cm from its top and bottom ends, using the hinge maker's inset template for the edge distance.
3. Mount the plates on the outer side and the divider to match, set back by the door's thickness for inset: door bottom at 12.1, top at 84.9.
4. Hang the doors flush with the frame's front and adjust the hinges for an even 0.3cm gap all round and between the pair. Check the frame is square first; any twist shows as a tapering gap.
5. Fit each knob 4cm from the door's inner edge and 6cm down from its top.

## 9. Install

Uses: 2 anti-tip brackets, wall fixings.

1. Remove the skirting boards.
2. Tilt each unit up with at least two people; it is heavy. Standing up, its corner sweeps 215cm, so check the ceiling.
3. Fix each unit's anti-tip bracket through its top panel into a stud wherever possible (find them with a stud finder). Where there's no stud use spring or snap toggles, never plain plugs. Never skip this.
