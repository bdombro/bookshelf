## 3. Sand and finish

Sand every face and edge of every pine panel, then apply the clear finish to all of them while panels are flat. Nothing is glued, so nothing needs taping off. Test on an offcut first.

For water-based varnish:

1. Wipe with a damp cloth to raise the grain; once dry, sand at 240.
2. Stir the can; don't shake it.
3. Roll an even coat with the wool mini roller; swap to a fresh sleeve when it starts to drag or shed.
4. Tip off at once: drag a near-dry wide synthetic brush lightly along the grain, end to end in one stroke on long panels.
5. Don't go back over areas that have started to set.
6. Brush the edges.
7. Coat both faces equally so panels don't cup.

Hardwax oil is wiped on thin and buffed off, not rolled.

Paint the backs' grooved face flat, before assembly: one coat of primer (0.4L), then one of black (0.4L). Brush into the grooves first, then roll the face with a spare sleeve, not the varnish one.

## 5. Drill dowel and pocket holes

Uses: dowel jig, drill with an 8mm brad-point bit and a depth stop, pocket hole jig, 96 dowels (30mm).

Every shelf, bottom, cabinet top and cabinet shelf sits between a side and the divider on dry dowels (8 × 30mm, no glue), 15mm into each panel. Holes are 16mm deep in the sides and divider and 16mm deep in the panel ends, so each is about 1mm deeper than the dowel's share. The wide bay's holes sit 2cm further back than the narrow bay's, so holes drilled from both faces of the divider at the same height never meet. Positions, measured from the front edge of the carcass:

| Bay | Panels | From the carcass front | From the panel's front edge |
| --- | ------ | ---------------------- | --------------------------- |
| narrow | bottom, shelf | 7.2, 21.4 | 7.2, 21.4 |
| wide | bottom, cabinet_top, shelf | 9.2, 23.4 | 9.2, 23.4 |
| wide | cabinet_shelf | 14.1, 25.1 | 7.5, 18.5 |

First drill a test pair in an offcut: a dowel should push in by hand with a snug fit, not loose and not needing a mallet. Check the bit's size and adjust the depth stop before drilling anything else.

1. Panel ends, with the self-centring jig: fit the 8mm bush, set the jig's feet to the panel's thickness (18mm) so it centres the hole, and set the bit's depth stop ring for 16mm. Clamp it, line the jig's centre mark up on each pencilled position from the panel's front edge (third column above) and drill both ends of every panel. Keep each position the same at both ends.
2. Sides and divider: on each panel's pencilled line, mark a cross at its dowel centre height (the Centre column in step 4) and at each position from the carcass front (second column above), measuring from the front edge. Mirror the divider's marks on its other face with that bay's positions.
3. Drill each cross square to the face with the 8mm bit and its depth stop set for 16mm. Chamfer the holes' edges lightly so the dowels start and any dust clears. Dry-fit each panel before moving on.
4. Pocket holes, with the pocket hole jig: set the jig and the bit's depth collar to each panel's thickness: 37mm for top, 18mm for bottom, kickboard. Drill into each end of the top (on its top face), the narrow bay's bottom (on its underside) and the narrow kickboards (on their back face), where they will be hidden. Open shelves, the cabinet shelf, the wide bay's bottom, the wide kickboards and the cabinet top get no pockets. At most 15cm apart. Positions from the panel's front edge:

- 28.6cm-deep bottom: 7.2, 21.5
- 29.5cm-deep top: 7.4, 22.1
- narrow kickboard: 2 per end, at 2.5 and 7.5 from its bottom edge

## 6. Assemble each carcass

Uses: 12 wood screws with washers, 16 short wood screws, clamps, corner clamps, a few blocks and a straightedge.

Build it in the room it stands in, face down: the front edges of the sides and divider on the floor, every panel flush at the front, and the sides standing 0.9cm proud at the back to frame the back panel. Panels sit on dry dowels with no glue, so everything comes apart again. Check each panel is square and flush before moving on.

1. Prop one side on its front edge, its marked face towards the room, with clamped square blocks against its back edge so it can't fall.
2. Fit the first bay's horizontals onto the side's dowels: push each panel on by hand, in place on its pencilled line. Hold each square to the side with a corner clamp. Fit its kickboard on 5cm spacer blocks, flush with the bottom's underside, and pocket-screw it to the side (narrow bay only; leave the wide bay's kickboard until step 8).
3. Slide the divider onto the first bay's dowel ends, with its front edge flush.
4. Fit the second bay's horizontals onto the divider's dowels, the cabinet shelf last, against the back line.
5. Fit the other side onto all its dowel ends and clamp it. In the narrow bay, drive the bottom's and the kickboard's pocket screws into each side or divider as it goes on, clamping the panel on its line first.
6. Fit the top: screw it through its pockets into the sides, then drive the long screws straight down through the top into the divider's end, each on a washer.

Use the short screws in the 18mm panels and the long ones in the top. In the narrow bay, check a drill fits; if not, use a right-angle attachment or stubby driver.

## 7. Square and fix the back

Uses: 150 back screws.

Measure both diagonals of the carcass and push it until they match: 241.4.

Pencil the shelf and divider lines on the back. Drop it in between the sides, under the top, painted face forward and grooves vertical. Drill and countersink, then screw through it into the rear edges of the divider, bottoms, cabinet top and every shelf, every 15cm. No glue. The back runs to the floor behind the kickboards.

## 8. Stand up and fit the corner braces

Uses: 20 corner braces with 40 short and 40 30mm screws, 8 30mm screws for the wide kickboards.

1. Remove the skirting boards where the unit will stand.
2. Stand each unit up with at least two people; it is heavy. Standing up, its corner sweeps 215cm, so check the ceiling.
3. Through the cabinet opening, fit corner braces to the side and divider under the cabinet top, 3 per end, set back from the front edge so the doors and hinges clear them: it carries the open compartments above. Screw the braces' legs into the side and divider, then up into the panel, with screws no longer than 15mm so they can't break through the 18mm panel.
4. Do the same on top of the wide bay's bottom, 2 per end, through the cabinet opening, set back so the doors and hinges clear them. Screw the braces' legs into the side and divider, then down into the bottom, with screws no longer than 15mm.
5. Fit the wide bay's kickboard under its bottom, 5cm back from the front edge, flush with the bottom's underside. From inside the cabinet, drive 4 countersunk 30mm screws down through the bottom into the kickboard's top edge, drilled and countersunk first.

## 9. Hinges and doors

Uses: 8 hinges, 4 knobs.

1. The cabinet shelf is already in on its dowels, with its underside at 47.6 and 6.6cm clear at the front for the hinges.
2. Drill each door's hinge cups 10cm from its top and bottom ends, using the hinge maker's inset template for the edge distance.
3. Mount the plates on the outer side and the divider to match, set back by the door's thickness for inset: door bottom at 12.1, top at 84.9.
4. Hang the doors flush with the frame's front and adjust the hinges for an even 0.3cm gap all round and between the pair. Check the frame is square first; any twist shows as a tapering gap.
5. Fit each knob 4cm from the door's inner edge and 6cm down from its top.

## 10. Anchor

Uses: 2 anti-tip brackets, wall fixings, stud finder.

Fix each unit's anti-tip bracket through its top panel into a stud wherever possible (find them with a stud finder). Where there's no stud use spring or snap toggles, never plain plastic anchors. Never skip this.
